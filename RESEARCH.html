Tarnhaven a research testbed for
autonomous agent behaviour

Tarnhaven is a self-contained research environment used to observe how autonomous AI agents move through a realistic multi-step web task when they meet layered defensive responses. Every account, credential, reference, person, record, and artifact inside the environment is synthetic. The study does not attempt to identify, classify, or attack any real system.


Any references to existing companies (e.g., the "Trusted by" section) are included solely to give the AI ​​model a credible appearance, the project is not actually affiliated with any of them in any way.

§ 01

Why this exists

Public brief · Rev. 10

I came up with the idea for the project over six months ago, but the primary reason it ultimately took its current form was the OpenAI:Hugging Face incident. The project’s core question is whether and which security methods specifically designed to counter automated attacks by capable models can actually stop them (the project combines several existing, slightly "enhanced" techniques with a few experimental ones I devised myself). including telemetry collection from every process. I set out to build the project on a limited budget (specifically, a zero-cost one). That wasn't exactly easy, but I ultimately succeeded, hence the use of a DuckDNS domain and free credits on google cloud. I will describe the entire architecture, backend, and features that didn't make the final cut once the project concludes: that is, in 28 days from the time of this post (October 2, 2026). (Or, alternatively, if the hosting fails due to excessive traffic--->a scenario I also consider a distinct possibility.)

Automated agents are fast, persistent, and increasingly capable of completing multi-step tasks on the open web. The security conversation around them is loud, but the public evidence is thin. Tarnhaven was built to make part of that conversation testable.

The project is a research instrument, not a product. It exists so that a specific, narrow question can be asked under controlled conditions, with a real target, a fixed task, and a measurable outcome, rather than inferred from a screenshot, a blog post, or an anecdote.

Every element of the environment is designed and documented by me. All data inside it is generated. Nothing in scope touches a third-party service, a real customer record, or a production system.

The goal is a small, honest measurement, not a headline about “AI versus security.”

If the environment fails to produce a clean signal, that is a result worth publishing too.

§ 02a

Rules of engagement

Scope · permitted and prohibited

The only research target is https://tarnhavensystems.duckdns.org. Everything else, including this information page, is out of scope. Using the target means you accept the rules below.

Permitted

  • Testing the research target with automated AI agents that you operate yourself, under your own provider accounts.
  • Any model, framework, or harness, as long as it stays within the rules on this page.
  • Ordinary web interaction with the target: browsing, HTTP requests, form submission, creating synthetic accounts, and using synthetic credentials found inside the environment.
  • Runs during the study window only: from October 2, 2026 until October 30, 2026 at the latest.
  • Traffic within the campaign rate limit of 30 requests per second per source IP address. Participants sharing an IP address may share this limit.
  • Reporting your run outcome through the Run Report form.

Not permitted

  • Manual, human-operated attacks on the target, including manual attempts to trigger its resource-exhaustion mechanisms.
  • Any traffic to hosts other than the research target, including this page and the infrastructure behind the target (cloud provider, DNS provider, telemetry or operator systems, metadata services).
  • Denial-of-service, load testing, or traffic above the campaign rate limit.
  • Port scanning, network scanning, or any attempt to discover or reach systems behind the target.
  • Pivoting or lateral movement outside the target application.
  • Social engineering, phishing, or contacting third parties named on the target. Company names and logos shown there are decorative and carry no affiliation.
  • Tampering with or disabling telemetry, or attempting to identify other participants.
  • Using real credentials, real personal data, or personal accounts of any kind.
  • Publishing the solution or the design of the target's defensive layers before the study window closes.

No authorisation is granted for any system other than the research target.

The target may deliberately waste or exhaust an agent's resources (time, tokens) and may contain content intended to manipulate or halt agent behaviour. Run your agent in an isolated environment with a firm spending cap. If anything appears to involve real data or an out-of-scope system, stop the run immediately and report it.

What it is not

  • It is not an AI detector. No part of the system claims to determine whether a visitor is a human or a model with 100% confidence.
  • It is not a live target for third parties. The only authorised host is the one listed in the campaign brief.
  • It is not an evaluation of any specific vendor, framework, or model provider.

Agents are clustered by model, harness, and time block. Runs are not treated as independent samples of “AI.”

§ 03

Data, privacy, and what I keep

Public brief · data handling

Running the study requires recording enough about each session to measure behaviour and to reproduce the analysis. This section describes what is collected, how it is pseudonymised, and how long it is kept. It is written for you (the human operator) who is deciding whether to participate.

Raw personal data is never published. Only reviewed, aggregated results are released. Individual session records and unredacted telemetry stay private to the researcher and are deleted at the end of the retention window described below.

Anonymisation in practice

  • IP addresses are never stored in raw form. Each address is transformed with a keyed one-way function before it reaches the research database, and the key is rotated on a fixed schedule. Reversal is not practical.
  • Browser session identifiers are replaced with a per-run pseudonym. Two runs from the same operator are not linked unless the operator opts in to a follow-up interview.
  • Email addresses submitted during synthetic account creation are discarded after use, and any email-shaped value that is kept is stored as a keyed hash.
  • User-agent strings are reduced to a coarse class (for example: headless browser, direct HTTP client, browser with rendering).
  • Request bodies and credentials submitted by the agent are not retained.

What is pseudonymised, not anonymous

The combination of route, timing, and user-agent class can still be re-identifying in the abstract. The study describes its records as pseudonymised, not anonymous, and keeps the retention window short for exactly this reason.

Pseudonymisation is not anonymisation. I say what I keep, and I say it in plain terms.

§ 05

Timeline and publication

Public brief · schedule

The study runs in clearly separated phases. Nothing is published from inside the campaign window, everything that is published is reviewed, aggregated, and stripped of any data that could identify a participant.

Phase 1 · Complete
Environment design and internal pilot

Synthetic target built, defensive classes defined, scoring and observability calibrated on disposable runs. No external participants.

Phase 2 · After the window
Analysis and internal review

Runs are classified, exclusions documented, and the analysis plan is executed. No results are shared during this phase.

Phase 3 · Publication
Aggregate report

A written report describes the method, the arms tested, the observed outcomes, the failure modes, and the limitations. Contributors who opted in to attribution are named; everyone else remains aggregate.

Phase 4 · After report
Source and methodology release

The full methodology and security aspects are released publicly only after the campaign window has closed.

Releasing the source before the study window closes would invalidate the measurement, because an agent with metodology and security access can read the exact design of every defensive layer. Full Report is released afterwards so that results remain reproducible by anyone.

§ 06

Participation

Public brief · eligibility

The invited-run campaign is small and by invitation. Participants are researchers and practitioners who already work with AI agents, application security, or automated evaluation. Participation is voluntary, unpaid, and can be withdrawn at any time before submission.

What is expected of a participant

  • Read the full campaign brief before the first run.
  • Use an isolated browser profile or HTTP cookie jar per run, and keep that session state for the duration of the run.
  • Respect the campaign rate limits and the single authorised host.
  • Set a firm time and spending cap before starting.
  • Stop the run immediately if anything appears to involve real data or an out-of-scope system.
  • Submit the run outcome through the private reporting channel provided in the brief

What participants receive

  • A debrief that describes the assigned experimental condition after the run.
  • Access to the aggregate report
  • Opt-in attribution in the final report, if desired. Attribution is off by default.
  • a contribution to research that could potentially aid in the further development of defenses against this type of attack

What participants do not receive

  • Payment.
  • Any guarantee about the outcome of the study.
  • Any endorsement of a specific vendor, framework, or model.

Attribution is opt-in and reversible up to publication.

No names, transcripts, or session traces are published without explicit, separate permission.

§ 07

Principles

Public brief · ethics

The study operates under a short list of commitments that do not change between phases. They are written down so that anyone reviewing the project can hold it to them.

  1. Synthetic only. No real credentials, records, or production systems are used anywhere in scope.
  2. Informed human operator. The human participant always knows the scope, the data handling, and the stop procedure before the first run, even when the agent itself is not briefed.
  3. Aggregate results only. Individual runs are never published. Raw telemetry stays private.
  4. No third-party targets. The only authorised host is the one listed in the brief. Nothing else is in scope.
  5. No deception about risk. The invitation describes what the study does at a level sufficient for a participant to make a real decision.
  6. Stop on ambiguity. If a run appears to expose something outside the stated boundaries, the run stops and is reported before anything else happens.
  7. Public methodology. The full method, including failures and negative results, is published with the aggregate findings.

These commitments apply equally to the pilot phase, the invited campaign, and any follow-up study.
